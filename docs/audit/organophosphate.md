# Audit: organophosphate.ts

**Severity: NONE (no definite errors found). One of the most carefully evidence-graded cases in the set.**

## What was checked
34M farmer, occupational OP exposure, HR52 BP92/58 RR28 SpO2 86% GRBS172, miosis, bronchorrhoea, fasciculations, plasma cholinesterase 800 IU/L (ref 4,300–11,500) — coherent cholinergic crisis.

- Atropine correctly taught as titrated to drying of secretions/clearing chest, explicitly **not** to heart rate or pupil size (both can remain abnormal for reasons independent of adequate atropinisation) — this is the case's stated central teaching point and is accurate, high-value teaching.
- Pralidoxime correctly `requiresFirst: ['atropine']` (oxime does nothing for the muscarinic crisis) with correct time-criticality reasoning about enzyme-inhibitor "ageing" into an irreversible complex. The rationale text also candidly and correctly notes that the Cochrane evidence base for pralidoxime is weak/mixed (no clear trial-proven mortality benefit, one cohort showing more intermediate syndrome in the pralidoxime arm) while still correctly presenting it as standard/conventional practice — an accurate and unusually well-calibrated evidence caveat rather than an overclaim.
- Plasma (pseudo)cholinesterase vs. RBC (true) acetylcholinesterase are correctly modelled as two distinct enzymes with different kinetics (plasma falls/recovers fast; RBC enzyme mirrors the synaptic one and recovers slowly, ~1%/day) — accurate and an important, often-conflated distinction.
- Succinylcholine correctly graded **harmful**: correctly reasoned that OP poisoning inhibits the same plasma cholinesterase that normally metabolises succinylcholine, producing prolonged paralysis; a non-depolarising agent (rocuronium) is correctly specified for intubation instead.
- Morphine/aminophylline/phenothiazines correctly grouped as harmful, each with a distinct, correct mechanism (respiratory depression/secretions; seizure/arrhythmia risk; sedation/seizure-threshold lowering).
- Decontamination (clothing removal, soap-and-water wash, staff PPE) correctly modelled as running in parallel with, never delaying, antidote therapy — correct and addresses a genuine secondary-exposure risk to staff.
- Intermediate syndrome is correctly modelled as a distinct, delayed (around 24–96h post-exposure, consistent with the case's "two days later" framing) neuromuscular complication presenting with neck-flexor weakness and respiratory muscle involvement, distinct from both the acute cholinergic crisis and any residual sedation — accurate and a genuinely difficult, high-value teaching point.
- The incidental self-harm/psychosocial-stress finding, correctly flagged as needing direct suicidal-ideation screening rather than being assumed away, reflects real-world epidemiology of OP poisoning in South Asian agricultural settings.

## Sources consulted
- Standard toxicology teaching on organophosphate poisoning (atropinisation endpoint, pralidoxime mechanism and timing, succinylcholine contraindication, intermediate syndrome), cross-checked against my knowledge of the Cochrane systematic review on oximes for OP poisoning (insufficient evidence of benefit) — domain knowledge, internally consistent with the case's own careful evidence framing; no web search needed as the case's claims were specific, correctly hedged, and consistent with well-established toxicology teaching.
