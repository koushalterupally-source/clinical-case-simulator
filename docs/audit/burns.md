## What was checked
34M, 70 kg, 40% TBSA flame burn with inhalational injury signs (hoarseness, singed nasal hair, soot, carbonaceous sputum), 40 min post-injury.

- TBSA arithmetic: face/neck 9% + anterior chest/abdomen 18% + right upper limb 9% + front of right thigh 4% = 40%, matching the stated total and consistent with standard rule-of-nines segment values.
- Parkland/ABA fluid calculation: 2 mL × 70 kg × 40% TBSA = 5,600 mL/24h, arithmetic correct; correctly timed from time of injury (not arrival) for the first 8-hour half — a commonly-missed and correctly-taught point. The case explicitly and correctly flags the 2011 ABA reduction from the classic 4 mL/kg/%TBSA Parkland figure to the now-recommended 2 mL/kg/%TBSA starting rate, while accurately noting many Indian textbooks/question banks still print 4 mL — a genuinely well-handled debatable-convention callout rather than an error.
- Carboxyhaemoglobin 32% (reference <3% non-smokers, <10% smokers) with correct teaching that standard 2-wavelength pulse oximetry cannot distinguish COHb from oxyhaemoglobin (SpO2 97% is falsely reassuring) — accurate and clinically important. COHb falling to 14% after high-flow 100% O2 is physiologically plausible (100% O2 shortens COHb half-life from several hours on room air to roughly 40–90 minutes).
- Serum lactate 4.1 mmol/L correctly raises concern for concomitant cyanide exposure in enclosed-space smoke inhalation — a genuine and often-missed teaching point.
- K+ 5.1 mEq/L correctly attributed to tissue injury; CBC haemoconcentration (Hb 16.8, Hct 50%) correctly attributed to early plasma volume loss.
- Sequencing: morphine `requiresFirst: ['airway_intubation']` with a harmful-sequence penalty for sedating a patient with impending airway compromise before intubation — correct and important teaching.
- Escharotomy correctly indicated for circumferential full-thickness chest/limb burns based on clinical findings (reduced chest wall movement, diminished right pulses), not waiting for pulses to disappear.
- Prophylactic broad-spectrum antibiotics correctly graded **harmful** (not standard of care in burns without documented infection; promotes resistance) — correct, matches current burn-care teaching (topical antimicrobials + surveillance preferred over blanket systemic prophylaxis).
- Tetanus prophylaxis, NG decompression, active warming (avoiding prolonged cold-water first aid for a large burn, unlike a small burn) are all correctly reasoned.
